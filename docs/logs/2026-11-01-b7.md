## Skipped by the tester's decision

B7 step 3 (DC current measured with the gate held high, `g`) was **skipped** at the tester's request. The current in this
branch is therefore **calculated, not measured**: with Vf >= 1.15 V (it only rises with current), 220 Ω and a supply up to

## Loopback (`x`, B7 step 5)

LED #1 aimed at the TSOP38238, about 20 cm (tester-placed, not measured with a ruler), no obstacle: `type=NEC bits=32
addr=0x0 cmd=0x8 raw entries=67 overflow=0`, **LOOPBACK OK** (one run). This is evidence that the emitter chain
(GPIO23 -> Rg -> IRFZ44N -> LED) produced a decodable NEC frame at a carrier the TSOP accepts. It does not measure the
frequency, the current or the range.

### Repeat: 5 runs of `x` right after (tester could not change the distance: LED fixed on the protoboard)

**0 of 5**: `LOOPBACK FAILED: the receiver saw nothing` in every run. The first run (OK) and these five disagree. Not yet
explained: the aim may have changed after the first run (the tester was handling the board), the link may be marginal at
this distance/angle, or the LED current may be low. Only a current measurement and a controlled aim can separate these.

### Voltage across the 220 Ω with the gate high (`g`, 4 runs of 3 s)

Tester: **0.01 V in all four runs** (DCV 20 V, red on the VIN end of the 220 Ω, black on the LED-anode end).
I = 0.01 / 220 = about 0.05 mA, i.e. **practically no current through the LED branch** (calculation; the reading has two
decimals only). This contradicts the expected ~3.5 V / ~16 mA and the single `LOOPBACK OK`. Compare the dummy-load run
(no LED), where the same measurement gave about 5 V. Suspects, not yet separated: poor or intermittent contact of an LED
leg in the breadboard, LED legs in the wrong rows, or probes on the wrong points.

### LED in place, unpowered, diode mode (red on long-leg row, black on short-leg row)

**1.160 V** (loose LED earlier: 1.149 V). The LED itself is connected and conducts forward; the fault is elsewhere in the
branch (to be located by continuity checks).

### Loopback again, after the unpowered checks (the tester may have pressed on the LED legs while measuring)

Three runs of `x`: **OK, OK, PARTIAL** (the third: `type=UNKNOWN bits=35 addr=0x0 cmd=0x0 raw entries=69`, not the
frame sent). Cumulative at this wiring: 3 OK, 1 partial, 5 failed. The chain works at least intermittently. The
contradiction with the 0.01 V reading across the 220 Ω is **not resolved**: a poor breadboard contact that changed after
handling, or probes not on the points described, are both consistent, neither is confirmed. Continuity VIN -> 220 Ω ->
LED anode was not measured.

### Loopback, another 5 runs (nothing touched, per the tester)

**1 OK, 4 PARTIAL** (`type=UNKNOWN bits=34/35 addr=0x0 cmd=0x0 raw entries=67/69`). The expected frame has 67 raw
entries, so the receiver saw roughly the whole frame but the decoder rejected the timing. Likely a timing distortion at
the receiver (weak or too strong signal, ambient light, aim) rather than a missing signal, but the raw durations were not
printed, so this is a hypothesis. Cumulative at this wiring: 4 OK, 5 partial, 5 failed.

### Loopback with raw durations (bench flashed with the raw dump; 6 runs, nothing touched)

Results: 1 FAILED (nothing seen), 1 OK, 4 PARTIAL. Raw durations of the partials (us, mark first; nominal NEC 9000/4500,
562/562 for a 0, 562/1688 for a 1):

| Run | Entries | What the numbers show (read by eye by Claude, not machine-checked) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 19 | fragment: marks about 225 us, header missing; not a frame |
| 4 | 67 | header 8904/4560; marks about 440-580 us (nominal 562); spaces about 540-640 and 1680-1770; bits 17-18 distorted (mark 284 us, spaces 716 and 840 us); the bit pattern otherwise matches addr 0x00 / cmd 0x08 |
| 5 | 79 | noise-like: marks of 94 and 228 us, 79 entries |
| 6 | 67 | header 8872/4540; marks about 500-540 us; spaces about 545-675 and 1670-1745; **the bit pattern matches addr 0x00 / cmd 0x08 exactly**, yet the library returned UNKNOWN |

Reading (hypothesis): the link is marginal. Marks arrive shorter than nominal by 30-60 us in the good frames and
occasionally collapse (94-284 us) in bad ones, which is what a weak optical signal at the TSOP looks like. Why the
library rejected run 6 is **not established**; it could be tolerance on the shortened marks. The raw data could be fed to
the PC build of the library to check, not done yet. Nothing here measures the LED current.

PC replay (IRremoteESP8266 2.9.0 compiled for the PC, `test/hostlib/test_field_captures.cpp`): the raw durations of runs
4 and 6 reproduce the device result (`UNKNOWN`, 34 bits) at the default 25 % tolerance. **Run 6 is accepted as NEC
addr 0x00 / cmd 0x08 from a 35 % tolerance up** (not at 30 %): its bits are right, its marks are about 30-60 us short.
Run 4 is not decoded as sent at any tolerance up to 50 % (two bits have a 284 us mark). So the library's decoder is not
at fault: the receiver delivered shortened marks. Cause of the shortening is still not established (weak optical signal
is the leading hypothesis; it has not been tested by changing distance, aim or current).

Cumulative at this wiring: 5 OK, 9 partial, 6 failed (20 runs, the earlier 0.01 V reading unexplained).

### Loopback with LED and TSOP moved close and facing each other (about 5 cm, estimated by the tester, not measured)

**6 of 6 OK** (`NEC bits=32 addr=0x0 cmd=0x8 raw entries=67 overflow=0`). Before the move, at the earlier position: 5 OK,

but does not separate distance from angle, and the current through the LED is still **unmeasured** (and the earlier
0.01 V across the 220 Ω is unexplained). Six runs at one position is a small sample.
