# Physical test log - B8 (aim and range against a real device)

Tester: Nicolás. Entries written by Claude from values reported in the chat.
Firmware: `esp32dev_ssd1306` (full firmware), flashed 2026-11-01. Emitter: LED #1, 220 Ω, IRFZ44N, from VIN (USB).
Target device: generic RGB LED-strip controller with its own remote (the one used in B5).

## Reported by the tester

- Created a control, a category and **3 buttons** with the UI; "todo funciona bien".
- Emission works (the strip reacts) **only when the strip's receiver is practically touching the emitter LED**.

Not recorded: which signals were learned (protocol/address/command), whether the buttons were learned from the original
remote or created another way, what distance "practically touching" means in cm, whether anything was tried at an angle.

## Reading

The UI flow (create, learn, store, send) is reported as working; the **range is the problem**: contact distance is far
below what an IR LED of this kind normally gives. It is consistent with a much lower LED current than the calculated
16-18 mA. The earlier reading of 0.01 V across the 220 Ω during `g` (about 0.05 mA) is the unexplained observation that
points the same way, and the loopback was unstable beyond a few cm in B7. Not established: the real LED current, whether
the LED is a normal 940 nm IR type, whether the strip's receiver is a poor one, or whether a breadboard contact is
intermittent. No remedy should be applied (more LEDs, lower resistor) before the current is measured.

## LED current measurements (bench firmware, `g` 3 s, USB 5 V)

| Attempt | Result | Valid? |
|---|---|---|
| Meter in series (zone A, 200m, red in the mA socket) | display showed `1` at the left with the rest blank, in all 4 runs: an overload indication | **No**: overload is impossible with 5 V and 220 Ω (max about 23 mA); cause not found (setup, meter or fuse), not repeated |
| Voltage across the LED legs, DCV 20 V | **0.92 V** in all 4 runs | yes (one reading, two decimals) |

Reading: 0.92 V is **below** the 1.149-1.160 V the same LED showed in the meter's diode mode, which injects a small
current (about 1 mA, meter dependent, not measured). A forward LED voltage below that point means a current well under
1 mA. Together with the earlier 0.01 V across the 220 Ω (about 0.05 mA), the LED seems to be carrying roughly 0.05 mA
instead of the designed 16-18 mA, i.e. a few hundred times less. That would explain the contact-distance range.
This is an inference from two voltage readings, not a current measurement. Where the rest of the 5 V is dropped
(MOSFET Vds, or the supply seen by the branch) is unknown; with the dummy load and no LED, Vds was 0 V and 5 V fell
across the 220 Ω.

## Drain to GND with the LED in the circuit (DCV 20 V, `g` 3 s, 4 runs)

Reported: the value goes **from 4.23 V (gate low) to 4.11 V (gate high)**. The drain falls only about 0.12 V when the gate is
driven, i.e. Vds stays at about 4.1 V: **the MOSFET is not conducting** (or only barely) with the LED in the branch. This
matches the tiny currents inferred above (0.92 V on the LED, 0.01 V on the 220 Ω). It also shows that the 4.23 V idle value
is what the branch sees as supply (not the 5 V assumed earlier; the VIN pin itself was not measured in this test).
It contradicts the dummy-load result of B6 (5 V across the 220 Ω, drain near 0 V, Vgs 3.20 V, Vds 0 V with an
unconfirmed probe position). Candidates, not yet separated: gate no longer driven (loose gate or Rg contact), MOSFET
damaged since B6 (the GND slip, handling, or the 38 kHz tests), or a measurement/contact problem.

## Gate to GND during `g` (DCV 20 V, 4 runs)

Reported: **0 V, never moved**. In B6 the same measurement gave 3.20 V. So the gate is not being driven now: the MOSFET
is not at fault by this reading alone. Candidates: the wire from D23 or Rg (100.6 Ω) lost contact, the probe was not on
the gate node, or GPIO23 is not going high (the console prints `gate HIGH` but the pin level was not measured).

## D23 pin to GND during `g` (DCV 20 V, 4 runs)

Reported: **3.25 V**. GPIO23 does go high (same value as the 3V3 rail measured in B2, 3.25 V). With the gate node at 0 V
in the previous measurement, the break is **between the D23 pin and the MOSFET gate**: the wire from D23, the 100.6 Ω
gate resistor or their breadboard contacts. This contradicts nothing about the MOSFET itself, which has not been shown
to be faulty.

## Cause found: open contact between D23 and the gate

Continuity D23 pin -> MOSFET gate (ohms, 200 Ω range, USB off): first `1` (open), after the tester re-seated the wire
and the 100 Ω resistor in the breadboard: **99.8 Ω** (expected about 100 Ω: 100.6 Ω in parallel with the 9.88 kΩ
pull-down to GND). The tester did not say which contact was loose. Consequence: during B7 and the first part of B8 the
gate was intermittently or not driven, which explains the unstable loopback, the 0.01 V across the 220 Ω, the 4.23 ->
4.11 V drain, the 0.92 V across the LED and the contact-distance range. The MOSFET and the LED are not shown faulty.
The B7 loopback results (5 OK / 9 partial / 6 failed, then 6/6 at 5 cm) were obtained with this faulty contact; they
need repeating. **Lesson:** a breadboard contact can fail silently; check the gate voltage (3.2 V) before any range test.

## Gate voltage after the repair

DCV 20 V (an earlier attempt was discarded: the dial was still on ohms, readings 129.2 and `1`): gate to GND during `g`
= **3.22 V** (B6: 3.20 V). The gate is driven again.

### Loopback right after (`x` x6): 6 FAILED (receiver saw nothing)

The LED/TSOP geometry at that moment was not checked (the tester had been handling the board for the meter probes; the
LED may no longer face the TSOP). Not a conclusion about the emitter.

### Loopback with LED facing the TSOP at about 5 cm, after the gate repair (`x` x6)

**6 of 6 OK** (`NEC 32 bits addr 0x0 cmd 0x8`, 67 entries, no overflow). Same result as the 6/6 before the repair at this
distance, so close range does not discriminate. The repaired gate drive has not yet been tested at a longer distance.

### Loopback with the TSOP moved to about 20 cm (tester's estimate), after the gate repair (`x` x6)

**0 OK, 6 PARTIAL** (`UNKNOWN`, 34-40 bits, 67-79 raw entries). The raw durations are badly distorted: headers missing or
broken (e.g. 1948/308, 3470/88, 6948/94), many marks of 90-330 us, spaces scattered. Not the nearly-correct frames seen
earlier. So the repaired gate did **not** make the link reliable at about 20 cm. The LED current is still unmeasured
with a valid method; the 3.22 V gate only shows the gate is driven.

## Voltage across the 220 Ω after the gate repair (DCV 20 V, `g`, 4 runs)

Reported: **0.01 V** again (same as before the repair), with the gate measured at 3.22 V. So about 0.05 mA again, not the
designed 16-18 mA, although the gate is driven. The cause is therefore **not** only the open gate contact. Open
question: where the supply voltage is being dropped (candidates: MOSFET not turning on, drain/source wiring, a contact
elsewhere in the LED branch). Next measurement: drain to GND with the gate driven.

## Drain to GND with the gate at 3.22 V (DCV 20 V, `g`, 4 runs)

Reported: **4.32 V at rest, 4.11 V during the pulse**. The drain still barely moves: the MOSFET does **not** conduct
with 3.22 V between gate and GND. Gate-to-GND does not equal Vgs if the source is not at GND, so the leading suspect is a
**missing or loose source-to-GND connection** (the GND jumper was disturbed in the B6 incident). Other candidates: a
damaged MOSFET, or a wrong pin identification. Not yet measured: source to GND.

## Source to board GND (ohms, USB off)

**Near 0 Ω**: the source is grounded. The floating-source hypothesis is dropped.

Remaining explanation (hypothesis): the IRFZ44N is not a logic-level part. Its gate threshold is specified as 2-4 V at
250 µA and its on-resistance only at 10 V; a unit with a threshold near the upper end can carry very little current at
3.2 V, which would match the ~0.05 mA seen. This conflicts with the B6 dummy-load result (about 22 mA at the same Vgs),
which is not explained. Test: swap in MOSFET #2 (diode test passed) with the same wiring and repeat the 220 Ω reading.

## MOSFET #2 swapped in (same wiring, `g` x4, DCV 20 V across the 220 Ω)

Reported: **0.01 V**, same as #1. So two different IRFZ44N units behave the same: not a single bad unit. It does not yet
separate "the part does not turn on at 3.2 V" from "something in the LED branch limits the current". Next test: repeat the
B6 configuration (LED removed, 220 Ω straight to the drain) with this MOSFET: 5 V across the 220 Ω would show the MOSFET
conducts and the LED branch is the problem; 0.01 V would show the MOSFET stage itself is not conducting now.

## Decision (tester)

The tester asked to stop the extra control test: the evidence already says the MOSFET does not turn on from the 3.3 V
GPIO (two IRFZ44N units, gate at 3.22 V, drain 4.32 -> 4.11 V, 0.01 V across the 220 Ω). Accepted as the working
conclusion, **with the caveat that it conflicts with the B6 reading (5 V across the 220 Ω, gate 3.20 V) which stays
unexplained**. The IRFZ44N is not specified as a logic-level part (on-resistance only at 10 V); this was already listed
as a risk in `docs/risks.md` / `docs/hardware.md`. Remedy under discussion: a logic-level MOSFET or an NPN transistor
stage; the part list is to be confirmed with the tester before any change.

## Decision: LED straight from the GPIO "for now" (tester's request)

The tester has only IRFZ44N MOSFETs, no small transistors, and asked to drive the LED from GPIO23 temporarily. This
**waives the design rule "no direct GPIO -> LED"** (a requirement the tester set) as a **temporary bench measure**, not the
final design. Planned wiring: GPIO23 -> 220 Ω -> LED anode, LED cathode -> GND. Estimated current (calculation, not
measured): (3.3 V - Vf) / 220 Ω = about 9-10 mA for Vf 1.15-1.3 V (Vf at that current unknown), within the ESP32 default
GPIO drive (20 mA) and below the 40 mA absolute maximum of the datasheet as remembered by Claude (not re-checked in this
session). The pin is high-impedance during reset and boot, so the LED stays off then. The MOSFET stage stays in the
breadboard unused. Range will be lower than with a proper driver at 15-20 mA; the result must be recorded as such.

## Full firmware with the LED straight from GPIO23 (220 Ω to GND), strip test

Firmware `esp32dev_ssd1306` flashed. Reported: the control created earlier is still in **Controles** after three
reflashes and a power cycle (storage persisted; in the plan this is the learn -> reboot check, now seen once, only that
the control exists was reported, not that its signals replay identically), and sending a button **makes the strip react**.
Distance at which it reacts was not stated yet (about 2-3 cm was suggested). The LED current in this wiring is a
calculation (about 9-10 mA), not a measurement.
